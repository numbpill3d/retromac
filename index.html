About the iMac G3

The iMac G3 was Apple's breakthrough all-in-one computer that single-handedly saved the company from bankruptcy. Released in August 1998, it featured groundbreaking translucent design in vibrant colors that made technology friendly and approachable.

Steve Jobs famously said the iMac would be "the computer for the rest of us" - and it delivered on that promise with unprecedented ease of use and stunning visual appeal.

The Story Behind the Revolution

In 1997, Apple was 90 days from bankruptcy. Steve Jobs had recently returned as interim CEO, and the company desperately needed a hit product. The result was Project C1 - what would become the iMac.

Jonathan Ive's design team took inspiration from everything around them: LifeSaver candies, kitchen appliances, even bathroom fixtures. The goal was simple - make the computer friendly, not intimidating.

💡
Design Philosophy: "The back of our computer looks better than the front of anyone else's." - Jonathan Ive
The iMac's impact extended far beyond computing: • First mass-market computer to eliminate floppy drives • Popularized USB as the standard connection • Influenced industrial design across all product categories • Saved Apple from bankruptcy with 278,000 units sold in first quarter • Created the template for future Apple successes (iPod, iPhone, iPad) The translucent aesthetic became so popular that everything from kitchen appliances to automobiles adopted similar design languages.
